Learning Journey of a Resource Teacher Vision
Abstract
What is RTV and what does she do?
  • Work alongside parents and caregivers, classroom teachers and teacher aides (early childhood, primary, intermediate and secondary), therapists and counsellors to provide resources for the students who are blind or have low vision
A typical day of an RTV
  • Read ophthalmic reports of students and do research to understand the eye conditions of each student
  • Communicate with parents/caregivers, teachers, therapists (emails, phone calls, messages) to arrange visits
  • Visit students and do vision assessments to determine the most appropriate learning media for the student (braille, large print)
  • If the student has additional needs, research the medical condition of the student and adapt the assessment and teaching techniques/materials
  • Write the reports in line with the mainstream NZ curriculum, Te Whāriki; the Early Childhood Curriculum and the Expanded Core Curriculum
Why lifelong learning?
  • Each child is an individual, and each child's development is unique. Children with the same condition develop differently depending on the environment in which they are raised.
  • The more knowledge you gain, the better you are at assessing students and delivering the services they deserve.
Why do I choose to be an early childhood RTV?
  • It is humbling to assist the families and the children who have a lot on their plates, especially in the early childhood sector.
  • I learned a lot about myself while learning to support them.
